Last month, the HEART-GeN Lab joined researchers, trainees, and colleagues from
around the world at the **American Society of Human Genetics (ASHG) 2026 Annual
Meeting**, held October 20–24 in Montréal, Canada.

<!--more-->

The meeting brought together the human genetics and genomics community to share
scientific advances, exchange ideas, and foster collaborations. For our lab, it
was an opportunity to contribute to conversations about analytical approaches in
human genetics, present ongoing research in brain epigenomics, and reconnect
with colleagues across the field.

This year's highlights included a workshop co-led by our principal investigator,
Dr. Kynon J. M. Benjamin, a lightning talk and poster presentation by Research
Data Analyst [Alexis Bennett, MS][alexis-bennett], and a poster presentation by
senior undergraduate researcher [Elisa K. Johnson][elisa-kain-johnson].

![Elisa K. Johnson, Alexis Bennett, and Dr. Kynon J. M. Benjamin in front of the ASHG 2026 Annual Meeting banner in Montréal]({{ site.url }}/assets/images/ashg-2026/ashg-2026-team.jpg)

## Exploring Modeling Choices in Human Genetics

On October 20, **Dr. Kynon J. M. Benjamin** co-led an interactive workshop with
**Dr. Minoli Perera** titled *"Modeling Choices in Omics: Implications for
Health Disparities in Human Genetics."*

The workshop focused on an important consideration in modern human genetics:
analytical decisions can influence scientific discovery, biological
interpretation, and the conclusions drawn from studies involving diverse
populations.

Through interactive exercises using simulated genomic data, participants
explored how choices involving ancestry representation, covariate selection,
statistical modeling, and the integration of results across populations can
affect genetic and multi-omic analyses.

The session encouraged researchers to critically evaluate the assumptions
underlying their analytical approaches and consider how different modeling
strategies influence statistical power, effect estimates, and interpretability.

These discussions reflect an important part of the HEART-GeN Lab's research
mission: developing and applying computational approaches that account for
molecular and population heterogeneity while supporting rigorous and
reproducible human genetics research.

[Learn more about the workshop](https://www.ashg.org/product/ashg-2026-modeling-choices/).

## Sharing Our Research in Brain Epigenomics

A major highlight of ASHG 2026 was the opportunity for HEART-GeN Lab members to
present ongoing research investigating genetic and environmental contributions
to molecular variation in the human brain.

### Alexis Bennett, MS: Investigating Genetic and Environmental Contributions to Brain DNA Methylation

**Alexis Bennett, MS**, a Research Data Analyst and member of our lab's
computational research staff, represented the HEART-GeN Lab through both a
lightning talk and a poster presentation.

On Wednesday, October 21, Alexis delivered a lightning talk during **Session 38:
"Genetic Variation: From Catalogs to Consequences."**

![Alexis Bennett at the podium during her ASHG 2026 lightning talk, with her acknowledgements slide on screen]({{ site.url }}/assets/images/ashg-2026/ashg-2026-alexis-lightning-talk.jpg)

On Thursday, October 22, she also presented a poster during the Epigenomics
session, titled:

**"SNP-based heritability distinguishes genetically anchored and environmentally
patterned methylation variation in the Black American postmortem brain."**

![Alexis Bennett beside her poster in the Epigenomics session at ASHG 2026]({{ site.url }}/assets/images/ashg-2026/ashg-2026-alexis-poster.jpg)

This research examines how genetic and non-genetic influences contribute to
patterns of DNA methylation in the human brain. By applying SNP-based
heritability analyses, the work investigates differences between methylation
variation associated with local genetic variation and patterns potentially
reflecting environmental or other influences.

The study contributes to efforts to better characterize brain epigenomic
variation in populations that have historically been underrepresented in genomic
research.

Alexis's presentations provided opportunities to share the lab's computational
research with the broader scientific community and engage in discussions about
ancestry-aware epigenomics and the molecular biology of neurological disease.

We are proud of her contributions to the lab's research program and her
participation in multiple scientific presentation formats at ASHG 2026.

### Elisa K. Johnson: Ancestry-Aware Perspectives on Neurological Disease Risk

**Elisa K. Johnson**, a senior undergraduate researcher in the HEART-GeN Lab
with an interest in pursuing genetic counseling, presented a poster during the
Complex Traits and Polygenic Disorders session on Thursday, October 22.

Her poster was titled:

**"Ancestry-Aware Analysis of Environmentally Influenced Brain DNA Methylation
Regions and Neurological Disease Risk."**

![Elisa K. Johnson beside her poster at ASHG 2026]({{ site.url }}/assets/images/ashg-2026/ashg-2026-elisa-poster.jpg)

Elisa's research examines how environmentally influenced patterns of DNA
methylation in the brain may relate to neurological disease risk, incorporating
ancestry-aware approaches to the analysis of complex molecular traits.

The project explores the importance of considering genetic ancestry and
environmental influences when studying molecular variation and
disease-associated genetic signals.

This work aligns with our lab's broader interest in understanding how different
sources of molecular variation contribute to neurological and psychiatric
disease while improving the representation of diverse populations in genomic
research.

Presenting at ASHG provided Elisa with an opportunity to share her work, engage
with researchers across human genetics, and gain experience communicating
complex scientific questions to a broader research audience.

![Dr. Kynon J. M. Benjamin and Elisa K. Johnson at her poster in the Complex Traits and Polygenic Disorders session]({{ site.url }}/assets/images/ashg-2026/ashg-2026-elisa-kynon-poster.jpg)

We are excited to support her continued development as a researcher and her
interest in connecting genetics research with future work in genetic counseling.

## Celebrating Science and Creativity Beyond the Lab

The week also included a special milestone for Elisa outside of human genetics.

The concert was held at the Bienen School of Music in Evanston, Illinois, with a

Elisa's accomplishments in both scientific research and musical composition

We were delighted to celebrate this achievement alongside her contributions to

## Reconnecting with Colleagues and the Human Genetics Community

Beyond workshops and research presentations, one of the most rewarding parts of
ASHG was the opportunity to reconnect with colleagues and members of the **Human
Genetics Scholars Initiative (HGSI)** cohort.

These conversations provided opportunities to exchange scientific perspectives,
reconnect with peers, and strengthen relationships within the human genetics
community.

Scientific conferences play an important role in building connections that
extend beyond individual projects and institutions. They also provide
opportunities for researchers at different career stages to share experiences,
learn from one another, and support continued professional development.

For the HEART-GeN Lab, these interactions reinforced the value of collaboration,
mentorship, and community in advancing scientific research.

## Reflecting on ASHG 2026

Our time in Montréal was an opportunity to share research, engage with new
scientific perspectives, and recognize the contributions of our team.

From Dr. Benjamin's workshop on modeling choices in omics to Alexis's research
presentations and Elisa's undergraduate research contributions, ASHG 2026
reflected the breadth of work and expertise within the HEART-GeN Lab.

We are grateful to the colleagues, collaborators, and members of the human
genetics community who connected with us throughout the meeting.

**We look forward to building on these conversations and continuing our work in
computational human genetics, brain genomics, and understanding the genetic and
environmental contributions to molecular variation and disease.**
